## Your home in the mountains

A small mountain lodge and restaurant dating back to 1943, in the roadless mountains above Ottsjö, Jämtland, Sweden.

## Where the road ends

Nearly eight kilometres from the nearest road, in the southern Åre mountains of Jämtland, stands Hållfjället. Built in 1943, this small mountain lodge has been given new life, bringing together hospitality, gastronomy and a deep affection for the surrounding landscape.

Guests arrive on foot, on skis or by snowmobile. Beyond the windows, the mountains stretch out in every direction. Inside, the table is laid, the sauna is warm and much of the character of the old house remains.

Hållfjället as it is today has grown out of a shared vision between its owners, Carl Hörberg and Maria Elgaard, and restaurateurs Linda and Rodrigo Pérez. An ambition to care for the history of the house while creating something that could not quite exist anywhere else.

Down in the village of Ottsjö, we grow our own vegetables, herbs and flowers. Fish, game, wild berries and mushrooms find their way into the kitchen, while the wine cellar brings together growers and landscapes from other parts of the world.

Life here is shaped by the seasons. By the long, light summer days, the first autumn frost and the snow that eventually settles around the house. Working in roadless country calls for ingenuity, careful planning and an ability to adapt.

At the heart of Hållfjället lies the contrast between the wild and the carefully tended. Between the vastness of the mountains and the intimacy of a small dining room. Between what nature gives us and what human hands can make of it.

> A place to spend time, rather than simply pass through.

## Since 1943

Hållfjället was built in 1943, and much of its original character remains. The small bedrooms, hand-painted woodwork, old bunk beds and creaking wooden floors all carry something of the house's history.

The house has been gently renewed, with care for its history and curiosity about what it might become. Old furniture, design classics, contemporary craftsmanship and objects collected over time sit comfortably alongside one another.

The former boiler room now houses the wine cellar. The loft has become a place for conversation, reading, a game of chess or something to drink before dinner. In the sauna, the view stretches out across the mountains.

There are ten small bedrooms, furnished with the original bunk beds, and shared bathrooms.

The house is still very much a mountain lodge. And that is precisely how we wish it to remain.

## Rooted in Ottsjö

Down in the village of Ottsjö, at the foot of the mountains, lies Hållfjället's kitchen garden.

Here, we grow our own vegetables, herbs and flowers. The northern growing season is short but generous, with long hours of daylight and cool nights. Each year brings new discoveries about what can be grown in this part of the world, and how far the season can be extended.

Even in late November 2026, with snow covering the ground and temperatures falling to minus sixteen degrees Celsius, there was spinach, kale, winter cress and Brussels sprouts to harvest. Beneath the snow, thyme retains its fragrance, while carrots rest in the frozen soil.

Much of the summer harvest is carefully preserved for the months ahead. Drying, fermenting, pickling and storing are as much a part of the work as sowing and harvesting.

The garden is not something separate from the restaurant. It is where much of the restaurant's work begins.

## In the kitchen

Dinner at Hållfjället begins long before anyone sits down at the table.

It begins with a seed in the garden, with a fishing trip, with the first mushrooms of late summer and with the work of the people who know this landscape.

Led by chef Rodrigo Pérez, the kitchen draws on the seasons, the surrounding mountains and the team's own harvest. Vegetables are given as much thought and attention as fish and game. At the heart of the kitchen stands the wood-fired oven.

The cooking is rooted in the place, but curiosity and inspiration are allowed to travel. Flavours, techniques and traditions from elsewhere find their way into the kitchen when they have something to contribute.

In winter, the pantry holds memories of summer. Berries, herbs, fermented vegetables and carefully preserved ingredients bring other seasons to the table.

> A seemingly simple dish may have an entire summer behind it.

## Where landscapes meet

In the old boiler room of Hållfjället lies a wine cellar shaped by years of curiosity about wine, its people and its origins.

Curated by sommelier and wine writer Linda Pérez, the collection has its heart in Champagne, Burgundy, the Loire, Jura and Savoie, with a particular affection for the vineyards of mountainous regions.

Wine is, of course, a matter of taste. But it is also agriculture, craftsmanship, cultural history and an expression of the relationship between people and their surroundings.

The cellar brings together wines from growers with a deep knowledge of their vineyards, from families who have tended the same land for generations, and from those who are finding new ways to care for old traditions.

Some bottles arrive in small quantities. Others are given time to mature.

At Hållfjället, one landscape is allowed to meet another. A wine from the French Alps may find its place beside a vegetable grown at the foot of the Swedish mountains.

> There is something rather beautiful in that meeting.

## The people behind Hållfjället

Hållfjället brings together people with a shared affection for hospitality, craftsmanship and life close to nature.

Restaurateurs Linda and Rodrigo Pérez both have backgrounds in Stockholm's restaurant world. Rodrigo's experience includes leading kitchens at Esperanto and Ekstedt, as well as working with the Bocuse d'Or team.

Linda studied restaurant and culinary arts at Grythyttan and has worked at Mathias Dahlgren and Esperanto. Alongside her work as a sommelier and restaurateur, she is a longstanding wine writer for Svenska Dagbladet and secretary of the Swedish Drinks Academy.

Together with Carl and Maria, and a small team of people with different skills and experiences, they carry the story of Hållfjället forward.

Working here means that the boundaries between the garden, kitchen, dining room and mountain are rarely fixed. The person preparing dinner may have spent the morning harvesting vegetables or bringing supplies up the mountain.

Everyone has a particular craft, but everyone is also part of something larger.

## Through the seasons

### Winter

Snow-covered mountain birches, blue winter light and ski tracks across the open fell. Long days outdoors give way to a warm sauna, candlelight and evenings around the table. As spring approaches, the days grow longer and the sun begins to warm the mountains again.

### Summer

Long, light evenings, flowering mountain meadows and paths winding through the landscape. Down in Ottsjö, the garden is at its most abundant. There is time for walking, fishing, gathering wild herbs and simply being outdoors.

### Autumn

The mountain birches turn golden, the forests fill with mushrooms and berries, and the air grows clear and cool. In the garden and kitchen, attention turns towards the winter ahead. A time for harvesting, gathering and preserving.

## Staying in the mountains

The journey to Hållfjället is part of the experience. Nearly eight kilometres of mountain trails separate the house from the village of Ottsjö.

Once here, there is time to settle in, warm up in the sauna, read a book or find a place by the window. In the evening, guests gather in the dining room for a five-course dinner shaped by the season.

After a night in one of the house's small bedrooms, breakfast is served before another day in the mountains.

For those wishing to explore the surrounding landscape, guided ski tours, ice fishing, dog sledding and other seasonal experiences can be arranged.

All stays are booked in advance. Snowmobile transport can be arranged during the winter.

> With just ten bedrooms, Hållfjället remains a small and intimate place.

### On skis to Hållfjället

We can think of few better ways to arrive at Hållfjället than on skis. Setting out from Ottsjö, Edsåsdalen or Trillevallen, we travel through old mountain forests, across snow-covered marshes and along trails with sweeping views of the southern Åre mountains. Accompanied by a guide, there is time to discover the landscape and pause for coffee brewed over an open fire.

### Ice fishing in the mountains

On one of the small mountain lakes surrounding Hållfjället, we spend a few hours with a fishing rod in hand, snow all around us and a hole leading down to the dark waters beneath the ice. We light a fire, brew coffee and hope to make the acquaintance of one of the mountain lakes’ Arctic char.

### By dog sled through winter

First comes the eager barking of the dogs and the sound of restless paws in the snow. Then we set off through mountain forests and open winter landscapes in the southern Åre mountains. A rather special way to travel, close to both the animals and the natural world. Along the way, we pause for coffee and something good to eat.

### Ptarmigan hunting in roadless country

An early morning, long before the mountains have quite awakened. The dogs are eager, the equipment is packed, and ahead lies a day in remote country where few people venture. Together with experienced dog handlers, and with the option of helicopter transport, we offer ptarmigan hunting on private hunting grounds.

*These are some of the ways to get to know the landscape surrounding Hållfjället. Other mountain experiences can also be arranged, guided by the seasons and the interests of our guests.*

## Hållfjället in brief

**Location**

Above the village of Ottsjö in the southern Åre mountains, Jämtland, Sweden.

**History**

Originally built as a mountain lodge in 1943.

**Accommodation**

Ten small bedrooms with original bunk beds. Shared showers and toilets.

**Getting here**

Nearly eight kilometres from Ottsjö, with approximately 300 metres of elevation gain. On foot in summer, on skis in winter. Snowmobile transport can be arranged.

**The stay**

Overnight accommodation, five-course dinner, breakfast and sauna.

**The kitchen**

Led by chef Rodrigo Pérez, with a focus on seasonal produce, the team's own kitchen garden, wild ingredients and preservation.

**The wine cellar**

Curated by sommelier Linda Pérez, with a focus on small growers, craftsmanship and wines with a distinct sense of place.
